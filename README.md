## About

Money laundering is the process of making illegally obtained money appear legitimate. Federal money laundering statutes (18 U.S.C. §§ 1956-1957) are among the most broadly applied in the federal criminal code, adding charges to virtually every case involving financial proceeds of criminal activity.

## Federal Money Laundering Statutes

### 18 U.S.C. § 1956 — Laundering of Monetary Instruments
The primary money laundering statute covers:
- Financial transactions involving proceeds of specified unlawful activity
- Transactions designed to conceal the nature, location, source, or ownership of proceeds
- Transactions to avoid reporting requirements
- International money laundering

### 18 U.S.C. § 1957 — Engaging in Monetary Transactions
Covers transactions of more than $10,000 derived from specified unlawful activity. This charge is easier to prove than § 1956 but carries lower penalties.

## Common Money Laundering Methods

### Structuring (Smurfing)
Breaking large cash deposits into smaller amounts to avoid Currency Transaction Report requirements. Structuring is itself a federal crime (31 U.S.C. § 5324).

### Real Estate
Using cash to purchase real estate, then selling to "clean" the funds. FinCEN Geographic Targeting Orders require reporting of all-cash real estate transactions in high-risk markets.

### Shell Companies
Creating layers of corporate entities to obscure the beneficial owner of funds. The Corporate Transparency Act now requires beneficial ownership reporting.

### Trade-Based Money Laundering
Over- or under-invoicing international trade transactions to move value across borders.

### Cryptocurrency Laundering
Using cryptocurrency mixing services, decentralized exchanges, and privacy coins to obscure the origin of criminal proceeds.

## Bank Secrecy Act

Financial institutions are required to file:
- **Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs)**: All cash transactions over $10,000
- **Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs)**: Transactions suspected of being related to criminal activity

Failure to file, or deliberately structuring transactions to avoid filing, is a federal crime.

## Who Is Prosecuted

Federal money laundering charges apply to:
- Drug traffickers laundering proceeds
- Fraud defendants concealing fraud proceeds
- Operators of money services businesses
- Bankers facilitating illegal transactions
- Professionals (lawyers, accountants) helping clients launder
